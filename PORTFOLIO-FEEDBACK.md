# Portfolio feedback & case study notes

Reference only — not wired into the site. Captured from review against what employers look for in art director / senior designer books, and how top AD portfolios tend to present work.

---

## Overall verdict

Strong place for a packaging / brand AD or senior brand designer hire: curated project count, real CPG work, intentional site craft. Where it still lags top AD books is **story and proof**, not taste.

Hiring rubrics roughly weight: **concept/strategy → craft → role/leadership → business impact → range**. This book is strongest on craft and range; weakest on strategy, role, and impact.

---

## What’s working

- **Six projects** — right range. Creative directors usually form an opinion in the first few pieces; 4–6 deep beats 15 shallow.
- **Clear lane** — packaging, structural, retail/gifting, brand systems for CPG. Recruiters trust you faster when the book has a point of view.
- **Real stakes / real names** — Grön, Pixlz, PR kits for Nike / Coca-Cola / NFL. Credibility matters more than speculative concept work at senior level.
- **Physical craft as a differentiator** — structural, fabrication, PR kits are rarer than another logo grid. Hireable signal for in-house brand / packaging leads.
- **Site restraint** — editorial type, quiet about, grouped services. Reads as someone who can hold a brand bar.

---

## Gaps vs top AD / designer portfolios

1. **Projects read as galleries, not case studies.** Top books answer in ~30 seconds: problem → insight → idea → system across touchpoints → your call → result. One-sentence blurbs + photos often get read as execution, not direction.
2. **Role is ambiguous.** Especially on team/brand work (Grön core, Halloween). Reviewers ask: art direct, design, both, own the system, manage vendors? One credit line closes that gap.
3. **Almost no outcomes.** Even soft proof helps: sell-through, SKU count, campaign duration, markets shipped, awards, press, retailer pickup. Business impact is increasingly scored.
4. **Services list is broader than the work proves.** Fine for freelance; for employment, a wide menu can dilute the headline. Strong AD sites usually imply services through 2–3 hero case studies.
5. **Design Toolbox is underbaked as a story.** Automation is a strong modern signal — but only if you show what friction it removed. Thin blurb + placeholder imagery is easy to skip.
6. **Thumbnail treatment vs packaging hiring reality.** B&W + grain is cohesive. For packaging roles, many CDs still want color/product truth visible at a glance on the grid (finish, material, shelf presence).

---

## Compared to top AD sites

Those usually share:

- One sharp positioning line
- 4–6 cases with light process crumbs (sketches, dielines, system boards — not walls of process)
- Cross-channel proof on at least 1–2 projects (pack → retail → campaign → digital)
- Easy contact + LinkedIn
- Optional: press, awards, select clients

This site is closer to a **strong specialist book with excellent production photography** than a **campaign AD book that sells thinking**. For in-house brand/packaging lead or senior designer, that’s often enough if a couple of projects deepen. For agency AD / CD tracks, concept narrative and leadership evidence matter more.

---

## Highest-leverage upgrades (priority order)

1. Turn **2 projects** (Pixlz + one Halloween or Grön core) into real mini case studies.
2. Add **one credit / role line** per project.
3. Add **one outcome** per project where you can (even qualitative).
4. Let **Work** carry the thesis; About can stay short.
5. Treat Toolbox as a systems/ops case, or demote it if imagery/story isn’t ready.

---

## Case study suggestions by project

Use a consistent skeleton for each:

| Section | What to answer |
| --- | --- |
| **Context** | Who / what / when (1–2 lines) |
| **Problem** | Business or brand problem, not “needed a cool pack” |
| **Insight / idea** | The strategic or creative call you made |
| **Your role** | Solo / lead / collaborator — be specific |
| **System** | How it lived across touchpoints (pack, retail, digital, merch) |
| **Craft proof** | 3–8 images that show range, not duplicates of the same hero |
| **Outcome** | Metric, soft result, or “what changed” |

---

### 1. Pixlz — *priority case study #1*

**Why deepen this:** Best “I can birth a brand” story. Independent brand outcome is rare proof.

**Suggested narrative beats**

- **Problem:** Grön’s existing lines weren’t reaching a younger / more playful customer segment (name the gap without junking the parent brand).
- **Insight:** Retro-arcade world as identity — not just a flavor line with a logo.
- **Your role:** e.g. brand identity + packaging system + art direction across launch (adjust to truth).
- **Show:** 3 SKUs as a system; color/flavor logic; one retail/event moment; one merch piece; website UI direction if you have a still.
- **Outcome ideas:** “Now operates as independent brand with own site + retail”; SKU count; launch markets; any sell-through or retailer feedback you can share.

**Good extras if you have them:** early concept boards, packaging family flat, before/after vs parent brand shelf.

---

### 2. Grön Halloween — Bite Club / Grön Arcana — *priority case study #2*

**Why deepen this:** Strongest “campaign AD” evidence — worlds, not refreshes; pack + merch + microsite.

**Suggested narrative beats**

- **Problem:** Annual LTO needs to feel event-worthy and distinct year over year, while still reading as Grön.
- **Idea (year 1):** Bite Club — vampire secret society as the world.
- **Idea (year 2):** Grön Arcana — tarot ritual + interactive reading mechanic (show you didn’t repeat yourself).
- **Your role:** packaging systems, campaign direction, illustration, UI/interaction direction — clarify what you owned vs collaborators (dev, photo, etc.).
- **Show:** one hero pack per year; merch; microsite/UI stills; maybe one pitch-deck slide as “how it sold internally.”
- **Outcome ideas:** multi-state retail footprint; campaign window; engagement on digital tarot if you have it; “two consecutive years of LTO creative ownership.”

**Structure tip:** One case with two chapters (2025 / 2026), not two separate projects — shows range and consistency of leadership.

---

### 3. PR Kits & Corporate Gifts

**Why it matters:** Big-name clients + concept-to-fab. Risks looking like a moodboard of objects without a throughline.

**Suggested narrative beats**

- **Frame as a capability case**, not six unrelated products: “From brief → concept → fabrication → approval for national brands.”
- **Pick 2–3 hero kits** (e.g. Wyoming Whiskey, American Metal, TD desk) and give each a mini problem: occasion, audience, constraint (budget, timeline, brand guidelines, unbox moment).
- **Your role:** concept, product design, client direction, fabrication oversight — be honest about shop vs design.
- **Show:** closed kit → open reveal; material detail; display in context if you have it.
- **Outcome ideas:** client names (already strong); reuse/reorder; event it shipped for; any “used at X” line.

**Avoid:** laundry list of every industry. Curate hard.

---

### 4. Structural Packaging & Fabrication

**Why it matters:** Differentiator vs pure graphic designers. Proves you think in 3D, materials, production.

**Suggested narrative beats**

- **Problem:** Product/kit components that don’t sit well in stock packaging — fit, protection, unbox, cost.
- **Process (light):** dieline → material tests → prototype → CNC/laser files → final build. 3–4 images max of process.
- **Your role:** structural design + hands-on fab background informing file prep (that lineage is a strength — say it once, clearly).
- **Show:** Zippo / wood box / awards as *examples of the same craft*, with one sentence each on constraint (fragile, premium, multi-piece).
- **Outcome ideas:** reduced damage, fewer assembly steps, cost vs aesthetic tradeoff you made, production-ready handoff.

**Tone:** engineering + craft, not “I like wood.”

---

### 5. Grön — Core Brand & Marketing

**Why it matters:** Shows you can run a living brand system under compliance — senior in-house signal. Risks looking “maintenance” without framing.

**Suggested narrative beats**

- **Problem:** Four product lines (Pearls, MEGA, Pips, Chocolate) need coherent brand presence across pack, POP, digital — under multi-state cannabis regs.
- **Idea:** System thinking — how updates stay on-brand while meeting market-by-market rules.
- **Your role:** packaging artwork, print proofing, digital/retail marketing adaptations — clarify ownership level (lead designer at Grön belongs here).
- **Show:** line family shelf/group shot; one compliance-aware pack detail; one POP or vinyl; one digital placement.
- **Outcome ideas:** number of SKUs/markets touched; cadence of updates; “sustained brand consistency across X channels” if metrics are thin.

**Don’t oversell as a campaign.** Position as **stewardship + systems** — that’s valuable for brand leads.

---

### 6. Design Toolbox

**Why it matters:** Modern ops / systems signal (automation, plugins, shipping tools). Weakest right now on imagery and proof.

**Suggested narrative beats**

- **Problem:** Repetitive production work eating time (name 1–2 concrete tasks — e.g. retail panel artboards).
- **Solution:** ExtendScript / Figma plugins / small apps (Cursor, GitHub, Supabase, Vercel) — show 1–2 tools, not a resume of tech.
- **Your role:** built for personal workflow; note if others used them.
- **Show:** before/after of a task; UI of plugin; screenshot of script output; maybe a short Loom later — but for the site, stills of the tool + result.
- **Outcome ideas:** time saved per job; fewer errors; “hours back per week for taste/direction.”

**Decision:** Either make this a real mini case with screenshots, or park it until assets exist. Placeholder photography undermines the whole book.
